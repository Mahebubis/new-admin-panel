WhatsApp tracking · journeys vs campaigns

Why the campaign counts your click and the journey doesn’t

Both are looking at the same tap. They are answering two different questions, and only one of those questions can be answered by somebody who is logged out.

The two questions

Every tap on a tracked link lands in the same place. What differs is what each report asks about it.

Campaign

“Did anybody tap?”

  • Counts rows in wa_link_clicks, grouped by a key stored in the browser.
  • Nobody has to sign in. The tap on its own is enough.
  • Moves the second you tap.

This is why your campaign shows clicks immediately.

Journey

“Which student tapped?”

  • Counts messages in journey_messages carrying a first_clicked_at stamp.
  • A stamp needs a name, and a logged-out tap has none.
  • Moves when the person becomes known — instantly if the link carried their id, otherwise at sign-in.

This is why your journey sat at zero.

So why not just copy the campaign?

Because that number is not quite what it looks like. The journey did count this way once, and it was taken back out.

The campaign de-duplicates taps using a random key kept in the browser’s storage. On an ordinary browser that works: one person, one key, however many taps. But WhatsApp opens links in its own in-app browser, and that browser starts with empty storage every single time. So every tap arrives wearing a brand-new key:

visitor:a3f1 visitor:9c02 visitor:41be visitor:d7a8 visitor:2e55 visitor:88fc visitor:b104 visitor:6ad3

Eight taps by one tester read as eight people. That is exactly what happened when the journey counted this way: a single person testing produced a report showing 13 clicks. An inflated number is worse than a missing one, because nobody can tell it is wrong. So the journey went back to counting only what it can prove.

Worth knowing about your campaign numbers too: the campaign’s Clicked figure is closer to “taps” than to “people”. It is not wrong — it is answering the easier question.

What I changed in the report

Zero was accurate and unhelpful at the same time. The taps were being recorded all along; they simply had nowhere to appear.

The Clicked tile now carries the unnamed taps underneath it — counted as taps, and labelled as taps:

Nothing is inflated and nothing is hidden. When that person signs in, the tap moves up into the real number on its own.

Three ways to get a named click

Choose by how badly you need it to happen before the sign-in.

Approach Named at the moment of the tap? What it costs
Link in the message body
the TRACKED_LINK variable		 Yes — logged out, instantly The link is visible in the text. Works today, already built.
Button, named at sign-in
what you have now		 No — within 7 days of the tap Nothing at all. Conversions are unaffected either way.
Button with a variable URL
Meta Cloud route		 Yes — logged out, instantly Meta app connected to the WABA, a newly approved template, and that step leaves Netcore.

Why the button cannot do this on its own: Netcore accepts a per-message button value and then never passes it to Meta, so WhatsApp rejects the whole message. A per-person link has to travel in the message body — or go through Meta directly.

Where the numbers live — wa_link_clicks: one row per tap, named only once known. journey_messages.first_clicked_at: the journey’s Clicked column. campaign_attributions: conversions, for campaigns and journeys alike.
